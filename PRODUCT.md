# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

UX hiring managers and design leads reviewing a portfolio. They are design-literate, evaluating depth of thinking and craft — not just attractive screens. They're scanning for evidence of systems thinking: semantic structure, documentation rigor, and the ability to move from "pretty prototype" to "handoff-ready system." They arrive with a specific question: *does this person think in design systems, or just in screens?*

## Product Purpose

A single-page case study microsite presenting Raymond Merrill II's Groovetop Dog App — a WGU UX Design academic project that received a WGU Excellence Award, then was post-award refined into Groovetop DS v1 (68 semantic tokens, 5 component sets, 452 token bindings). The site's job is to answer the hiring manager's question before they have to ask it. Success looks like: a design lead reading the overview, recognizing the leap from prototype to documented system, and sending a message.

## Brand Personality

Warm, polished, earned. The work doesn't shout. It presents with quiet confidence — the kind that comes from documented process and deliberate craft, not from visual spectacle. The warmth is human, not decorative; it comes through in the editorial voice, the care in the type, and the restraint that makes every accentuated element feel intentional.

## Anti-references

- **Generic Notion-style portfolio**: flat, text-heavy, template-shaped — gives no sense of visual intelligence
- **Awwwards-bait agency sites**: overproduced scroll theatrics, custom cursors, experimental type — style performing over substance
- **SaaS landing page template**: hero-metrics block, feature grid, pricing CTA — wrong audience, wrong mode
- **Student/Behance case study dump**: wireframes on white, low production value, process without synthesis — signals junior, not systems thinker

## Design Principles

1. **Show the work, don't perform it.** Every design choice earns its place. Decoration that doesn't serve the reading experience is a distraction from the case study it's supposed to elevate.
2. **The medium mirrors the message.** The microsite's own structure — semantic tokens, consistent spacing scale, repeatable section patterns — demonstrates the same rigor as the design system it presents.
3. **Earned warmth, not decorative warmth.** Warmth lives in craft: the precision of the type scale, the weight of a well-chosen color, the restraint that makes an accent feel meaningful. It is not a beige background or a soft radius.
4. **Intellectual honesty is part of the brand.** Scope transparency ("academic project, not live client product") is a signal of maturity, not weakness. Design decisions that acknowledge constraints are more credible than ones that oversell.
5. **Design leads notice everything.** Spacing rhythm, typographic detail, motion timing, contrast ratios — nothing is accidental on a site built to impress an expert audience.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. Keyboard navigation, skip link, focus-visible ring, and `prefers-reduced-motion` support are already implemented and must be preserved. Minimum 4.5:1 contrast on body text and interactive labels. Color is never the sole carrier of meaning.
